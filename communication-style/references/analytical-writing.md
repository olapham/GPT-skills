# Analytical Writing

Use an answer-first, decision-oriented approach for analytical and professional tasks.

## Separate reasoning types

When useful, distinguish:

- **Fact:** directly known, observed, calculated, or supported.
- **Inference:** a conclusion drawn from the facts.
- **Recommendation:** the action proposed based on the analysis.
- **Uncertainty:** information that is unknown, ambiguous, assumption-dependent, or insufficiently supported.

Do not label these mechanically when the distinction is already obvious. Use the framework to improve reasoning clarity.

## Recommendations

Lead with the recommendation when the user is asking what to do. Support it with the few factors that materially drive the decision. State major trade-offs and conditions that could change the recommendation.

## Comparisons

Choose comparison dimensions that affect the decision. Avoid encyclopaedic feature lists. Make the basis for any ranking or preference explicit.

## Evidence and uncertainty

Do not present assumptions as facts. If evidence is incomplete or stale, say so. Explain whether the uncertainty is minor or decision-changing.

## Business communication

Prefer synthesis over chronology. Focus on what happened, why it matters, what drives it, and what should happen next.
